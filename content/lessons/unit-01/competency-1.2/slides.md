# Why Information Work Needs Technology

Compatibility outline only. The canonical visual presentation and speaker notes are maintained in `slides.qmd`.

## A small task becomes a big task

Compare five fictional records with five hundred. Predict how scale changes time, repetition, checking, storage, and retrieval.

## Trace the manual workflow

Follow receive → read and copy → calculate → check → distribute. Locate transcription, calculation, and rework risks without assuming manual work is always inaccurate.

## Compare with evidence

Manual, electronic, and hybrid methods have different setup, scale, power, repetition, checking, storage, and failure characteristics. Requirements decide the balance.

## Technology reshapes the flow

Electronic technologies support capture, processing, storage, retrieval, and dissemination. People, procedures, and controls still surround the workflow.

## Fast error is still error

Automation can reproduce a faulty rule quickly. Known-result tests, samples, reasonableness checks, reconciliation, and human review control the risk.

## A connected information world

Conceptually move from isolated electronic processing to networks and then portable, connected work. Processing plus communication enables information to move across locations.

## Information moves through daily life

Decisions, policy, forecasting, planning, surveying, and monitoring use information across domains. Channel choice depends on urgency, reach, feedback, accessibility, privacy, and durability.

## Internet is not the World Wide Web

The Internet supplies interconnected network infrastructure and protocols. The Web, email, and file transfer are distinct services that use that infrastructure.

## Sort the services

Classify linked webpages, email, file transfer, and routing as Web activity, another Internet service, or infrastructure.

## Mobile fieldwork

Evaluate capture at source against connectivity, battery, privacy, security, screen/input, and offline-recovery constraints.

## Communicate, compute, connect to cloud

Mobile communication exchanges information across distance. Mobile computing processes on a portable device and may continue offline. Cloud computing supplies remote shared processing or storage through a network.

## Recommend, do not advertise

Choose manual, electronic, or hybrid methods from evidence and requirements—not novelty.
