# Teacher notes: From Data to Useful Information

## Curriculum boundary

Teach data, information, data manipulation, information creation, characteristics and quality of information, value, and relevance. Do not introduce the formal input-process-output model; that belongs to competency 1.3.

## Core explanation

Data are recorded observations or symbols considered before their meaning for a particular purpose has been established. Information is produced when data are interpreted or processed so that they answer a question or support a decision. The same item can be data in one context and information in another, so require learners to state the user and purpose.

Useful quality criteria for this lesson are accuracy, completeness, relevance, timeliness, consistency, clarity, and fitness for purpose. Quality is not an all-or-nothing label: an item may be accurate but irrelevant, or relevant but too late to be valuable.

## Period guidance

### Period 1 — Activate and distinguish

Display: `31, 27, 29, 35, 30` and “The mean temperature for the five days was 30.4 °C.” Ask which is data and which is information, then change the purpose (“Which day was hottest?”) to expose the role of context. Collect initial explanations without supplying a definition first.

### Period 2 — Explain transformation

Model sorting, classifying, calculating, summarising, and comparing. Use a short attendance dataset and think aloud: identify the question, select relevant fields, perform a transparent calculation, and state an information claim without overstating the data.

### Period 3 — Practise quality evaluation

Give groups contrasting notices: one accurate but undated, one current but missing a location, and one complete but intended for another class. Learners identify which criterion fails and the consequence for a user.

### Period 4 — Practical

Use `practical.md`. Observe whether learners retain the original data, document transformations, check calculations, and separate evidence from inference.

### Period 5 — Assess and reflect

Complete Worksheet section D individually. Review answers using `answers.md`. End with: “One way context changes the value of information is…” Use responses to group learners for retrieval at the start of competency 1.2.

## Misconceptions to address

- Data are not necessarily numbers; text, images, sound, and symbols may also be data.
- Processing does not guarantee useful information; poor-quality input or an unsuitable method can produce a poor result.
- “Accurate” does not mean “useful for every purpose.”
- More data do not automatically produce better information.

## Inclusion and accessibility

Read tables aloud when needed, provide a large-print copy, and allow calculation by spreadsheet or calculator. Do not use real student marks, attendance, names, or other personal records; all supplied datasets are fictional.
