# Unit 1 Checkpoint — Competencies 1.1 to 1.3

Time: 50 minutes · Total: 40 marks · Calculator permitted

Answer every question. All organizations and records are fictional.

## Q1 — Data and information [6]

A repair desk records job durations of `18, 25, 21, 16` minutes.

a. Distinguish the four recorded values from the statement “The mean job duration was 20 minutes” for a supervisor reviewing performance. [3]

b. Explain two manipulations used to create the statement. [3]

## Q2 — Information quality and value [6]

A notice reads: “Network maintenance starts at 14:00.” It has no date, affected location, expected duration, or issuer.

a. Evaluate two information-quality problems and their likely consequences. [4]

b. State two additions that would improve its value to laboratory users. [2]

## Q3 — Selecting a workflow [8]

A warehouse manually aggregates 2,000 item movements from four locations each day.

a. Analyse two limitations of the manual method at this scale. [4]

b. Recommend a digital or hybrid approach, explaining one benefit and one control needed to prevent errors spreading at scale. [4]

## Q4 — Connected and mobile work [6]

a. Distinguish the Internet from the World Wide Web and give one non-Web Internet service. [3]

b. Field staff use tablets where connectivity is intermittent. Evaluate one benefit, one constraint, and one suitable response. [3]

## Q5 — Input–process–output [8]

A fictional centre records seats available as capacity minus occupied seats.

a. Construct a specific input–process–output model that identifies the user and purpose. [5]

b. Evaluate the model when occupied seats are greater than capacity. [3]

## Q6 — Time and appropriateness [6]

An automated system reports a dangerous temperature accurately, but the warning reaches the operator six hours later.

a. Explain how timeliness affects the information’s value. [2]

b. Give two reasons a computer is appropriate for continuous monitoring. [2]

c. Justify one control or human responsibility that must remain. [2]
